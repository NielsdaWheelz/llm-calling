Early on Saturday morning, Mara woke to the smell of bread warming in the kitchen. Her aunt had set a blue cloth on the table and placed a round loaf beside a jar of strawberry jam. A pot of tea rested near four painted cups. Sunlight reached the floor in long golden shapes, and a sparrow sang from the pear tree beyond the window.

The family planned to spend the afternoon in a meadow beyond the village. Mara folded napkins while her cousin Theo washed apples in a wide bowl. Their aunt made sandwiches with cheese, cucumber, and soft green leaves. Their uncle wrapped a small lemon cake in paper and tied it with yellow string. Everyone chose one thing to carry so that no basket became too heavy.

They left after breakfast and walked between fields bright with buttercups. Sheep grazed on the lower hill, and two horses stood beneath an old chestnut tree. Theo counted white clouds while Mara looked for flowers along the lane. She found daisies, clover, and one tiny violet. Their aunt asked her to leave most flowers growing for bees and for other walkers to enjoy.

The meadow lay beside a quiet pond where reeds moved gently in the breeze. The family spread their blue cloth beneath a willow and arranged the food in the middle. They poured cool water into cups, shared the sandwiches, and passed the apples from hand to hand. The lemon cake tasted bright and sweet. A few crumbs drew the attention of a bold robin, though it kept a polite distance.

After lunch, Theo lay on his back and described shapes in the clouds. One looked like a sailing boat, another like a sleeping rabbit, and a third like a tall hat. Mara drew the willow in a small notebook. Her aunt read a chapter from a favorite story, using a different voice for each character. Their uncle listened with his eyes closed and smiled at the funny parts.

Later, they walked around the pond at an easy pace. Yellow flowers grew near the reeds, and dragonflies flashed blue above the water. On the far side, they met an older couple feeding grain to a group of ducks. The ducks paddled close together, making neat ripples that widened until they reached the shore. Mara and Theo watched until the last grain was gone.

In the middle of the afternoon, a brief shower crossed the meadow. The family sheltered beneath the willow while the rain tapped softly on the leaves. Their aunt poured the remaining tea, which was still warm, and everyone shared the final slice of cake. Soon the clouds moved east, the sunlight returned, and drops shone on the grass like small beads of glass.

Before leaving, they shook the cloth, gathered every cup and wrapper, and checked the grass carefully. Mara placed the fallen willow leaves from her drawing beside the pond rather than taking them home. Theo found a smooth gray pebble, admired it for a moment, and returned it to the shore. They wanted the meadow to look as peaceful for the next family as it had looked for them.

The walk home felt shorter. Evening light softened the hills, and the sheep had gathered near a stone wall. In the village, warm scents drifted from several kitchens. A neighbor waved from her doorway and asked about the picnic. Theo told her about the rabbit-shaped cloud, while Mara described the dragonflies and the rain shining on the grass.

At home, they put the cups away and folded the blue cloth. Their aunt placed the remaining apples in a bowl for Sunday. Their uncle wrote the date of the picnic on a paper calendar and drew a tiny lemon beside it. Mara added color to her willow sketch, and Theo began a new picture of the bold robin. Everyone agreed that a simple afternoon had become a lovely memory.
